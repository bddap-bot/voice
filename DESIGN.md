# Voice stage

The set is a shallow room viewed straight on. A warm back wall fills the frame, two darker side walls converge toward it, and a low plinth holds the puppet in the visual centre. The puppet is drawn by a fixed camera, so the figure's feet always sit at 89% of the canvas height and the strip below them is the set's floor. At widths above 720 CSS pixels the canvas is 36% of the stage width, 32% from the left, and ends above the 90 CSS pixel input desk. At phone widths the canvas is 90% wide, 3% from the top, and ends at 89% of the first visual viewport. The safe rectangle is the canvas above the feet; it contains the whole projected figure throughout animation.

On laptop and TV, the delegation log is a narrow wing on the left wall and the display, which the figure points at, is a narrow wing on the right; each is a fifth of the stage width, inset 3% from its edge. For 45 seconds after an item from the hub arrives the display wing widens to 28%, which still ends left of the canvas; then it returns to its resting width. Authentication occupies the upper-left edge, no wider than the canvas's left edge, and the wings start 200 CSS pixels down, below it. Appearance and credit sit at the foot of the left wing and session time at the foot of the right. Text and image input is a one-row desk across the foreground, below the feet and in front of the plinth, which rises under the feet behind the transparent canvas; nothing in the desk may wrap.

At 720 CSS pixels wide or below, and on short coarse-pointer landscape screens, the first visual viewport is the uninterrupted puppet stage. The set continues below the fold into a compact control room: status and authentication, plinth details, input, display, and log appear in that order. The stage height is read from `visualViewport.height` at load and on each visual-viewport resize, never from the canvas or the clock.

The composition should answer two questions in every rendered frame: does anything sit directly behind the puppet, and does the whole page read as a stage? Headless Chromium projects the feet through the runtime camera and compares the rendered rectangles of status, authentication, appearance, credit, timer, input, display, and log with the safe rectangle at phone, laptop, and TV sizes, with the display at rest and widened; every intersection count must be zero, and on laptop and TV the canvas must remain above the input desk and be wider than both wings. The stage reading comes from one continuous room, converging walls, pools of light, a central plinth, and functional surfaces that belong to the set.
