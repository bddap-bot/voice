# Voice stage

The set is a shallow room viewed straight on. A warm back wall fills the frame, two darker side walls converge toward it, and a low plinth holds the puppet in the visual centre. The puppet is drawn by a fixed camera, so the figure's feet always sit at 89% of the canvas height and the strip below them is the set's floor.

Above 720 CSS pixels wide, and on any screen 540 CSS pixels tall or less, the stage is three columns: the delegation log on the left wall, the puppet in the middle, and the display, which the figure points at, on the right wall. A dotted grip on the inner edge of each wall panel resizes it by drag, arrow keys, or double-click to reset; the canvas takes the remaining width, and each panel is capped at 28% of the viewport width so the puppet always keeps room. A thin status line with the session time sits above the canvas, and the canvas ends above a desk strip that holds the credit and the controls card.

In portrait at 720 CSS pixels wide or below, the stage stacks: status line, puppet, display, delegation log, and a dock row for the controls card. A grip above each panel resizes its height, capped at 24% of the viewport height. Panel sizes are kept per layout in local storage.

All controls live on one card. Minimized, it shows only Mute mic and the expand button. Expanded, it groups Microphone, Appearance, Send to the hub, and Device: two columns on wide screens and a one-column sheet on phones, every button at least 44 CSS pixels tall. Before a token is saved, the card opens on its own to the token entry.

The composition should answer two questions in every rendered frame: does anything sit directly behind the puppet, and does the whole page read as a stage? Headless Chromium projects the feet through the runtime camera and compares the rendered rectangles of the status line, credit, minimized card, panels and grips with the safe rectangle and with each other at phone, landscape, laptop, and TV sizes; every intersection count must be zero, and on laptop and TV the canvas must end above the card and be wider than both panels. The stage reading comes from one continuous room, converging walls, pools of light, a central plinth, and functional surfaces that belong to the set.
